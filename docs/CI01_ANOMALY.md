# The One Real Lead: CI01 Click-Rate Rise Coincident with the USS Omaha Event

## What was found (and confirmed four independent ways — not dismissed)
At SanctSound station **CI01** (northern Channel Islands, 185 km from the reported event),
the click rate rises ~8–12% coincident with the event's predicted acoustic arrival
(06:02 UTC, 2019-07-16):

1. **Within-day permutation:** after/before step at t0, **p = 0.003** (survives Bonferroni
   for the 6 discovery tests).
2. **Change-point:** the CUSUM break lands **within 24 s of t0**.
3. **Not a diel cycle:** 3 control days at the same local clock time are flat/declining.
4. **Empirical null across 9 control days** (5,708 sliding windows): the event ratio 1.077
   sits at **p = 0.006**, beyond the 99th percentile.

This is the strongest anomaly the whole investigation produced, and it is statistically
real relative to CI01's own variability.

## What it is — snapping shrimp, not marine mammals reacting
Click spectra before vs after t0 are **identical** (broadband, peak ~7 kHz, same
peak-frequency distribution); only the RATE changed. At ~40 snaps/s continuously, peak
7 kHz, these are **snapping shrimp**, not dolphins (which peak 20–60 kHz in discrete
trains). So it is a shrimp snap-**rate** increase with unchanged character — not animals
echolocating in response to something.

## Mundane explanations tested — and the honest result
- **Diel/tidal daily cycle:** ruled out (control-day clock-times flat).
- **Tidal phase:** tested against NOAA Santa Barbara predictions. The event day's 06:00 UTC
  is ~1h40m into the ebb — but control day **2019-07-02 was at nearly the same tidal phase
  (~1h54m into ebb) and showed no rise**. So simple tidal modulation does **not** explain it.
- **Click-character change:** none (it's the same shrimp snaps).
- **Physical causation by the event:** implausible — a water entry 167–185 km away cannot
  drive shrimp at CI01.

## Honest verdict
A **genuine, quantified statistical anomaly that I could not attribute** to diel cycle,
tidal phase, or click-character change — and equally, one that is **not compelling as a UAP
signal**, for three reasons:
1. **Single station** — CI04 (167 km) and CI05 (170 km) show no matching rise.
2. **It's shrimp**, whose snap rate is environmentally driven; a distant event cannot cause it.
3. **Look-elsewhere effect** — across this investigation I ran many tests (multiple stations,
   metrics, events, methods). A single p≈0.006 result is roughly what chance produces given
   that many comparisons. The follow-up tests show the rise is real *for CI01*, but they do
   not overcome the multiplicity of the search that found it.

So: real, unexplained, but most likely a coincidental environmental fluctuation — interesting,
not extraordinary. It is neither dismissed nor oversold.

## The one test that would settle it: multi-event reproducibility
A single anomaly after many comparisons is weak; a **repeatable** one is not. The decisive
next step is to run the identical case-control click-rate test at the closest hydrophone for
**several independent documented transmedium/UAP water events**. If a coincident biosonar/
snap-rate rise recurs across independent events, it beats the look-elsewhere problem and
becomes a real phenomenon worth publishing. If it does not recur, this was chance. That —
not more analysis of this one event — is what would make or break the lead.
